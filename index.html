Vendor cards — portfolio, gaps, recent moves

Full-stack giants

NVIDIA accelerator + stack

  • Has: data-center GPUs (Blackwell GB200/300 → Rubin 2026; AWS deal >1M GPUs thru 2027), NVLink/Spectrum-X networking, Jetson (Orin family → Thor robotics module), DRIVE Thor, DGX Spark ($3,999 desk-side), RTX Spark/N1X Arm PC SoC (fall 2026; laptops from Microsoft, Asus, Dell, HP, MSI, Lenovo — MacBook-Pro-tier pricing per Morgan Stanley), Isaac GR00T humanoid reference robot (Unitree chassis, Jun 2026), CUDA/NIM/Omniverse software moat.
  • Gaps: no phone silicon; no MCU; no memory; no low-power floor ($25+ Jetson territory only); premium pricing everywhere; camera ISP not prosumer-grade.
2025–26: RTX Spark PC entry; Jetson Thor humanoid push; $3.5B into MediaTek; Rubin ramp; FY28 growth capped by memory supply per CFO.

AMD x86 + FPGA

  • Has: Ryzen client CPUs with XDNA NPUs (≈50 TOPS class), Ryzen AI Max "Strix Halo" (up to 128 GB unified memory — runs 120B-param models locally; ~$1,500 minis, $3,999 Ryzen AI Halo dev platform), EPYC (Turin → 6th-gen 9006), Instinct MI355X → MI400 (HBM4, Jul 2026), Xilinx FPGA/adaptive SoC (Versal AI Edge), Ryzen Embedded V3000 + Ryzen AI Embedded P100/X100 (2026).
  • Gaps: no phone SoC or modem; no automotive line of consequence; no sub-10W edge floor; ROCm still trails CUDA; no memory.
2025–26: MI400 launch; Strix Halo ecosystem momentum (GPD Win Max 3 $1,750); Ryzen AI Embedded for industrial edge.

Qualcomm mobile-first platform

  • Has: Snapdragon 4→8 Elite mobile; X-series PC; Digital Chassis auto (cockpit leadership); XR/wear; QCS/QRB/Dragonwing industrial+robotics; best-in-class modems; new Dragonfly data-center bid (C1000 CPU + AI300 inference; Meta/Microsoft); Arduino (acquired Oct 2025).
  • Gaps: (see Aug 31 analysis) no CUDA-class open stack; no dGPU; no training silicon; no cheap floor; Linux openness weak outside boards; ~$280 flagship ASP squeezing mid-market to MediaTek.
2025–26: Arduino, Dragonfly, Dragonwing IQ8/IQ10/IQ-X; Sept 1, 2026 price hike; 2nm flagship Sept 22.

MediaTek volume mobile + consumer

  • Has: Dimensity 9500 flagship + unmatched sub-$10→premium volume tiers; Genio edge MPUs; Kompanio Chromebook; Pentonic TV; Filogic WiFi/BT; Dimensity Auto Cockpit CX-1/CY-1 with licensed NVIDIA RTX GPU IP (Infineon NOR qualified Aug 2026).
  • Gaps: no high-end GPU of its own (licenses IP); no data-center silicon; no MCU; no modem leadership vs Qualcomm; enterprise/dev tooling thinner than Android consumer stack.
2025–26: NVIDIA invests $3.5B (Aug 31, 2026) — edge-to-cloud + automotive platform alliance; Dimensity 9500 wins incl. Samsung Galaxy Tab S12 Ultra.

Apple captive vertical

  • Has: A-series (iPhone/iPad), M-series (M5 2025 → M6 first 2nm Mac chip, M5 Ultra — Aug 2026), Neural Engine NPUs (≈38+ TOPS class), MLX/CoreML, total SoC+OS+device integration.
  • Gaps: not a merchant vendor at all — nothing to buy; no external ecosystem (hence ○); no automotive/industrial.
Relevance: sets the perf/W benchmark every suite is judged against; M6/M5 Ultra on TSMC 2nm.

Samsung vertical + merchant memory

  • Has: Exynos (2600: first 2nm GAA, NPU +113% vs 2500), ISOCELL image sensors, foundry, DRAM/HBM/NAND — the most vertically integrated merchant player after Apple.
  • Gaps: Exynos merchant business is thin (mostly internal + a few licensees); foundry yield struggles vs TSMC; no PC/data-center logic franchise; no edge-dev ecosystem.
2025–26: Exynos 2600 in Galaxy S26 class; HBM race vs SK Hynix/Micron/CXMT.

Broadcom ASIC + networking

  • Has: custom AI accelerators for hyperscalers (TPU-class programs — the "other Nvidia" of DC), Tomahawk/Jericho switch silicon, WiFi/BT (Apple supplier), storage/industrial miscellany.
  • Gaps: no merchant edge SoC, no MCU, no visible dev ecosystem — an infrastructure component vendor, not a platform vendor.
2025–26: AI ASIC demand supercycle; market debates ASIC share gains vs Nvidia.
Industrial & embedded majors

NXP auto + industrial

  • Has: S32 automotive MCU/MPU; i.MX 95 (≤2 TOPS NPU, Mouser-stocked Jul 2026, huge SoM ecosystem: Gateworks, Variscite, ADLINK SMARC/OSM); Layerscape; Kinetis/LPC MCUs; EdgeLock secure elements; TEF8388 3rd-gen RFCMOS imaging radar (576 channels, L2+–L4).
  • Gaps: NPU ceiling ~2 TOPS — cannot chase GenAI edge; no consumer; no data center; no modem.
2025–26: i.MX 95 ramp + safety-island positioning; radar leadership push.

Texas Instruments analog + embedded

  • Has: the broadest analog/power portfolio in the industry (incl. data-center power), Sitara AM6x MPUs (secure-boot ecosystem growing, e.g. CommScope PRiSM), C2000 real-time, MSP430/SimpleLink wireless, Jacinto TDA automotive vision (DSP-first philosophy), mmWave radar.
  • Gaps: no high-TOPS NPU (deliberate: DSP+efficiency bet, misses the NPU marketing wave); exited phone APs long ago; no consumer platform.
2025–26: analyst re-rating on AI data-center power demand; steady industrial/MCU cadence.

STMicroelectronics MCU + low-power MPU

  • Has: STM32 — the reference MCU ecosystem; STM32MP2 MPUs (≈1.35 TOPS NPU); new STM32MP21 cost-tier MPU (Jan 2026); automotive safety portfolio.
  • Gaps: NPU ceiling ~1.4 TOPS; nothing above edge-HMI class; no connectivity leadership.
2025–26: MP21 broadens the low-cost MPU line for cost-aware edge.

Renesas auto MCU + vision

  • Has: automotive MCU leadership (R-Car cockpit/ADAS), RZ/V-series vision-AI MPUs, RZ/G3E quad-core MPU+NPU for HMI (SoM ecosystem: Virtium et al.), RA/RX MCUs.
  • Gaps: consumer and PC/DC absent; NPU TOPS modest vs Jetson/Hailo class; slower cadence than consumer vendors.
2025–26: RZ/G3E ecosystem build-out; auto consolidation plays.
Edge, vision & challenger silicon

Rockchip cheap + open

Host CPU IP — the layer above the edge

SiFive RISC-V core IP + DC host

  • Has: the broadest commercial RISC-V core portfolio (Essential / Performance / Intelligence XM / Automotive) plus system IP — IOMMU Gen 2, Shield, WorldGuard. P550/P570 Gen 3 with modern RVA23 support (May 12 2026), pitched at edge AI + high-end consumer. BigSky SF-2U870 datacenter dev platform (Aug 24 2026): 32× P870-D @ 2.0 GHz, TSMC N6, 256 GB DDR5-5600, 4× PCIe Gen5 x16 (64 lanes) + Gen3 x4, x16 double-wide GPU slot, 2× 7.68 TB U.2 NVMe, 10/25 Gb OCP 3.0, Ubuntu 26.04 LTS. $400M raise at $3.65B (Apr 9 2026).
  • Gaps: an IP licensor, not a chipset vendor — no NPU, ISP or codec of its own, so nothing here ships as a camera/audio SoC. BigSky is a porting/validation box for hyperscalers, not a product CPU. No hobbyist-priced board above HiFive Premier P550.
Why it matters: SiFive is deliberately accelerator-agnostic — NVIDIA NVLink Fusion adoption (Jan 15 2026, "bringing our coherent NVLink to the RISC-V ecosystem" — Jensen Huang) and an AMD ROCm 10.0 demo on the same BigSky (Sep 15 2026: P870-D head node + Radeon AI PRO R9700, Gemma4-E2B). Two hostile stacks, one neutral RISC-V head node.
The CUDA-on-RISC-V gate: at Hot Chips 2026 (Aug 23, Frans Sijstermans) Nvidia specified what a RISC-V CPU must do to host CUDA — RVA23, Server SoC + Platform specs (RAS, security processor), vector w/ predication, ACPI (BRS ratified 2025), PCIe coherency as a guarantee (the RISC-V spec only recommends it), and PCIe peer-to-peer; plus unpublished items — "the list fits within two pages". Host ≠ device: this lets a RISC-V CPU feed an Nvidia GPU; it does nothing for RISC-V GPUs/NPUs. An edge SoC like SpacemiT K3 scores 2 of 6 (it needs pci=noacpi to boot its NVMe). Net effect: RVA23 compliance is now two-tier — necessary for the Nvidia server path, ~irrelevant at the edge.
Memory (the cost driver)

CXMT (ChangXin) DRAM #4

  • Has: DDR4/DDR5, LPDDR4X→LPDDR6 mass production started ahead of Samsung/SK Hynix (Aug 2026); HBM3E small-batch/risk production (Alibaba T-Head and Cambricon testing; scale planned 2027); record IPO July 2026; revenue +874% YoY H1'26 riding the AI-memory shortage.
  • Gaps: no NAND; no logic; HBM yield/stack maturity far behind Hynix/Micron; sanctions exposure on tools.
Why it matters to builders: memory is now ~SoC-priced (LPDDR5X+UFS combo ≈ Snapdragon 8 Elite Gen 5 cost). CXMT's aggressive capacity = downward price pressure on commodity DRAM for China-supplied products — a tailwind for your BOMs even as HBM scarcity inflates data-center costs.
